Every campaign, scene, label, quote, and plot detail in this article is synthetic. None comes from an actual table or transcript.

A campaign archive can be complete for years and still contain holes.

One session has a transcript, a summary, a timeline, and a list of every important character. The next has three rushed bullets. Another has only a title. One page is empty, but the session before it ends at a locked gate and the session after it starts with the Party already inside.

The obvious AI prompt is:

> Read the sessions before and after this one and reconstruct what happened.

That prompt is also how you get a confident, polished, completely false account of a duel nobody remembers, a bargain nobody made, and a magic item that now appears to be canon because the prose sounded authoritative.

The difficult part is not writing connective prose. Language models are very good at that. The difficult part is controlling what kind of connection the model may make.

This is a prompt-design problem about bounded creativity. The goal is not to recover the lost session. That may be impossible. The goal is to produce the most useful account that the available evidence permits, while keeping facts, deductions, possibilities, and atmosphere visibly separate.

I ended up treating the task less like creative writing and more like constrained data interpolation:

- Direct notes define the spine.
- Neighboring sessions define the boundaries.
- Stable campaign records resolve names and persistent state.
- Every claim gets a confidence level.
- Missing facts stay missing.
- Prose may add texture, but not events.
- Nothing becomes canon until a human approves it.

Once those rules are explicit, the model can write something rich without pretending that richness is evidence.

## This is interpolation, not restoration

The word _reconstruct_ creates the wrong expectation. It suggests that one correct version exists and that enough reasoning can recover it.

Usually, that is not true.

Suppose Session N ends with the Party outside a sealed Chapel. Session N+2 begins with the Party inside, one doorway blocked, and the Scout absent. Session N+1 is empty.

Several things may be certain:

- The Party entered the Chapel before Session N+2.
- The state of at least one doorway changed.
- The Scout became separated from the visible group before Session N+2 began.

Many more things are only possible:

- The Party forced the main door.
- The Party found another entrance.
- Someone inside opened the door.
- The Scout entered first.
- The Scout left after the Party entered.
- The blocked doorway was changed by the Party.
- The blocked doorway was already in that state.

A normal storytelling prompt tries to select the most plausible path and turn it into a scene. A constrained interpolation prompt refuses to select when the evidence does not support selection.

That refusal is the feature.

The output can still be useful. It can describe the start state, the end state, the minimum transitions that must have happened, and the open space between them. It can also produce a readable non-canon narrative that uses careful language:

> By the next recorded session, the Party had crossed the Chapel's boundary. The surviving record does not show how. The Scout was no longer with the visible group, and one interior doorway could no longer be used normally. Whatever route carried the Party inside also changed the shape of the group and the space around them.

That is less exciting than an invented fight at the door. It is also honest.

## The three modes

Not every missing session has the same evidence. I use three modes, selected before any prose is written.

### Mode 1: boundary-only gap

Use boundary-only mode when the target session has no usable direct record.

Inputs:

- The end of the last reliable session before the gap
- The start of the first reliable session after the gap
- Stable campaign state that is relevant to those boundaries

The task is to identify the minimum state transitions required to move from boundary A to boundary B.

This mode has the least permission to narrate. It should produce:

1. Known start state
2. Known end state
3. Required transitions
4. Plausible but unconfirmed transitions
5. Contradictions or unresolved questions
6. A clearly labeled non-canon bridge

Boundary-only mode must not choose one route merely because it is dramatically satisfying. If three explanations fit, preserve all three or stay at the level they share.

For example, if the Party is outside a building before the gap and inside after it, the required transition is _entry_. It is not _breaking the west window_, even if a broken west window is later visible. Someone else may have broken it. The break may predate the Party. The Party may have entered elsewhere.

This mode is best for analysis and worst for authoritative narrative. It can suggest the shape of the missing session, but it cannot honestly restore the session.

### Mode 2: notes-first

Use notes-first mode when the target session contains useful direct notes and neighboring sessions are unavailable, weak, or unnecessary.

Inputs:

- Player notes
- GM notes
- A partial transcript
- A session outline
- Any artifact created during play

The direct record defines both sequence and scope. The model's job is to expand shorthand into readable prose without adding event-bearing facts.

If the notes say:

1. Party agrees to inspect the lower archive.
2. Scout takes the brass key.
3. Party enters through the service stair.
4. Alarm starts.
5. Session ends while the Party hides.

Then the prose must preserve:

- That order
- Those actors
- Those objects
- Those locations
- That unresolved ending

It must not add:

- Who proposed the plan
- How the Scout got the key
- What caused the alarm
- Where the Party hid
- Whether the guards saw them
- Whether anyone was injured

Notes-first mode sounds simple, but it is where models often become most dangerous. Clear bullet notes create a strong scaffold. The model then feels invited to decorate every gap between bullets with a cause, a reaction, or a tactical detail. Those decorations read naturally, so they are easy to miss during review.

The core instruction is:

> Expand only the language, not the event set.

### Mode 3: notes plus neighbors

Use notes-plus-neighbors mode when the target session has direct notes and the sessions around it are reliable.

This is the richest mode and the one that needs the clearest source priority.

The direct notes remain the spine. The previous session may explain the opening context. The next session may confirm the outcome or show where the missing session stopped. Neighboring sessions do not get permission to overwrite the target notes.

This matters because adjacent summaries often contain compression, retrospection, or mistakes. A later summary may say, "After escaping the Archive, the Party returned to the road." The target notes may show that the session ended before the escape and that the next session opened with the final part of it.

If the model trusts the later summary too much, it drags the escape backward into the missing session. It creates a neat arc and a false chronology.

The correct rule is:

> Target-session evidence controls what happened during the target session. Neighboring evidence supplies context, verifies continuity, and limits the ending.

When sources conflict, the output should not silently reconcile them. It should record the conflict:

- Target notes: Session ends during the alarm.
- Next-session summary: Opens after the alarm has stopped.
- Conclusion: The available records do not show whether the alarm stopped at the end of the target session or between recorded scenes.

This mode gives the best prose because it has both an internal sequence and external boundaries. It also creates the strongest temptation to over-infer. More context does not mean more permission.

## A tiered source hierarchy

Before writing, rank the sources. Do not give the model a pile of documents and tell it to "use all available context." That treats a rushed note written during play and a retrospective summary written months later as equivalent.

My default hierarchy is:

### Tier 1: direct evidence from the target session

- Transcript fragments
- Player notes
- GM notes
- Initiative or encounter log
- Images or handouts explicitly used in that session
- A session outline marked as completed during play

These sources define the event set. When two Tier 1 sources conflict, do not pick one without a reason. Record the conflict.

### Tier 2: immediate continuity boundaries

- The final reliable state before the target session
- The first reliable state after the target session

These sources constrain the start and end. They can prove that a transition happened within the gap, but they often cannot prove how it happened.

### Tier 3: repeated facts from nearby sessions

- A goal stated before and still active after
- A wound present before and after
- An item held consistently across sessions
- A relationship or location state confirmed several times

Repeated facts are useful for continuity. They should not be converted into target-session events. If the Party wanted to find the missing Cartographer before and after the gap, that does not prove they searched a specific room during the gap.

### Tier 4: stable campaign reference

- Character identities
- Known abilities
- Faction names
- Location structure
- Persistent inventory

Reference data can resolve what a noun means. It cannot prove that a known ability was used or that a known faction appeared.

Knowing that the Scout can turn invisible does not permit the sentence, "The Scout vanished and crossed the courtyard unseen."

### Tier 5: genre expectation and narrative convention

This tier may shape tone only.

It may help the model understand that a siege is tense, a funeral is solemn, or an unresolved chase should not read like a relaxed picnic. It may not supply facts.

Genre expectation is never evidence that:

- Guards fired arrows
- A crypt smelled of dust
- Rain fell during the journey
- A villain gave a speech
- The Party rested before entering

All of those details are common. None of them are free.

The hierarchy has one important override:

> Explicit target notes beat implied continuity from neighbors.

If a target note says the Party left through the north gate and the next summary casually calls it the east road, preserve the note and flag the mismatch. Do not rewrite the target note to make the archive look tidy.

## Turn the sources into hard constraints

Do not ask for prose first.

The model should first extract a constraint sheet. This changes the task from "tell a likely story" to "produce text that satisfies a verified set of conditions."

I divide constraints into seven groups.

### 1. Actor constraints

- Who is present?
- Who is absent?
- Who arrives or leaves?
- Are pronouns safely resolved?
- Is an action assigned to a specific actor, or only to the Party?

Do not promote a group action to a named actor. "The door was opened" is not the same as "the Scout opened the door."

### 2. Sequence constraints

- Which events have an explicit order?
- Which events only share a session?
- Which event was already in progress at the start?
- Which event continued into the next session?

Bullets are often chronological, but not always. The prompt should state whether list order is known to be temporal. If it is unknown, treat it as unknown.

### 3. Location constraints

- Where does the session begin?
- Which movement is explicit?
- Where does it end?
- Which locations are mentioned only as goals?

"They planned to visit the Chapel" does not place them in the Chapel.

### 4. Object constraints

- Who has each important object?
- Does possession change?
- Is the object used, carried, lost, or only discussed?
- What state is it in at the next boundary?

Objects are common sources of accidental invention. A model sees a key and a locked door and turns correlation into use.

### 5. State constraints

- Injuries
- Conditions
- Open quests
- Alliances
- Captivity
- Vehicle or building state
- Resource levels
- Active threats

The strongest interpolation facts are often state changes. If a lantern is intact before the gap and broken after it, it changed state somewhere in the gap. The cause may still be unknown.

### 6. Negative constraints

Negative evidence is easy to ignore and often very useful:

- No death is recorded.
- The missing person is still missing after the gap.
- The sealed chest remains sealed.
- The Party has not yet met the Curator.
- The combat continues into the next session.

These constraints prevent the model from resolving what must remain unresolved.

### 7. Style constraints

- Point of view
- Tense
- Desired density
- Allowed atmosphere
- Forbidden specificity
- Required non-canon label

Style is separate from truth. "Write it in rich prose" must never weaken "do not add facts."

A useful constraint sheet can look like this:

```markdown
## Hard constraints

- HC-01: The Party begins outside the Chapel.
- HC-02: The Party is inside by the next reliable record.
- HC-03: The Scout is not with the visible group at the next boundary.
- HC-04: One interior doorway is blocked at the next boundary.
- HC-05: The method of entry is unknown.
- HC-06: The cause of the Scout's absence is unknown.
- HC-07: The cause and timing of the blocked doorway are unknown.
- HC-08: Do not resolve the Chapel's central mystery.
```

The final text should be auditable against those IDs.

## Extract chronology and continuity before writing

The most reliable workflow uses two representations: a chronology and a continuity ledger.

### The chronology

The chronology stores only event order.

```markdown
| Order | Event                          | Source       | Confidence |
| ----- | ------------------------------ | ------------ | ---------- |
| 1     | Party reviews the route        | target notes | high       |
| 2     | Scout takes the brass key      | target notes | high       |
| 3     | Party uses the service stair   | target notes | high       |
| 4     | Alarm begins                   | target notes | high       |
| 5     | Session ends while Party hides | target notes | high       |
```

If two events have no known order, give them the same phase or state that the order is unknown. Never let prose impose a sequence that the source does not contain.

### The continuity ledger

The continuity ledger compares state at three points:

```markdown
| Element        | Before     | During                       | After                     |
| -------------- | ---------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Party location | courtyard  | service stair, lower archive | unknown                   |
| Scout          | with Party | takes brass key              | unknown                   |
| Alarm          | inactive   | starts                       | active or recently active |
| Brass key      | on hook    | with Scout                   | unknown                   |
| Central vault  | sealed     | not reached in notes         | still sealed              |
```

The ledger catches continuity errors that sound fine in prose:

- Returning an item that is still missing later
- Healing an injury that remains visible
- Finishing a fight that continues next session
- Moving the Party to a location they have not reached
- Revealing information that characters do not learn until later

This is also where the neighboring sessions are most useful. They are not there to donate scenes. They are there to define legal state transitions.

### Model the gap as a state transition

For difficult cases, I reduce each boundary to a state vector:

```text
S_before = {
  location: outside_chapel,
  party_members_visible: [Party, Scout],
  east_door: unknown,
  objective: enter_chapel
}

S_after = {
  location: inside_chapel,
  party_members_visible: [Party],
  east_door: blocked,
  objective: find_scout
}
```

The interpolation must explain only the delta:

```text
required:
  outside_chapel -> inside_chapel
  scout_visible -> scout_not_visible
  objective_enter -> objective_find_scout

observed but causally unknown:
  east_door_unknown -> east_door_blocked
```

This looks mechanical because it is. The mechanical pass protects the creative pass.

## Keep a confidence ledger

An uncertainty paragraph at the end is useful. A claim-level ledger is better.

I use four labels:

- **Recorded**: stated directly in target-session evidence.
- **Required**: not directly recorded, but necessary to connect reliable boundaries.
- **Supported**: consistent with more than one source, but not required.
- **Possible**: fits the evidence but competes with other explanations.

There is no "obvious" label.

"Obvious" is where invention hides.

Example:

```markdown
| Claim                        | Level       | Reason                            |
| ---------------------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------- |
| Party entered the Chapel     | required    | outside before, inside after      |
| Scout became separated       | required    | present before, absent after      |
| Scout entered first          | possible    | fits boundaries, not recorded     |
| East door was used for entry | possible    | door state changes, route unknown |
| Party fought guards          | unsupported | no direct or boundary evidence    |
```

The prose rules then follow the ledger:

- Recorded claims may be stated plainly.
- Required claims may be stated plainly with boundary framing.
- Supported claims need attribution or careful wording.
- Possible claims stay in an alternatives section, not in the main narrative.
- Unsupported claims are excluded.

This ledger also makes human review much faster. The reviewer does not have to fact-check every adjective from memory. They can focus on the claims that cross source boundaries.

## "No invention" does not have to mean lifeless prose

The first safe output often reads like a police report:

> The Party discussed the plan. The Scout took the key. The Party used the stairs. An alarm started. The Party hid.

It is accurate and unpleasant.

The answer is controlled atmosphere: expand the experience of known facts without adding new event-bearing facts.

There are three safe tools.

### 1. Rhythm

Sentence length can create pace without adding events.

> The plan was reviewed once more. The route did not change. The Scout took the brass key, and the Party committed to the service stair. Then the alarm began.

No new cause, gesture, or object has appeared. The rhythm gives the sequence weight.

### 2. Reframing known stakes

If the source already establishes that discovery is dangerous, the prose may carry that tension.

> Hiding was no longer preparation. It was the Party's immediate problem.

This restates the known state. It does not invent a hiding place or a pursuing guard.

### 3. Metaphor attached to known facts

> The alarm divided the session into two parts: the plan before it, and survival after it.

The metaphor changes presentation, not content.

### Unsafe atmosphere

Atmosphere becomes invention when it adds observable world facts:

- Weather
- Lighting
- Smell
- Architecture
- Crowd behavior
- Facial expressions
- Spoken dialogue
- Physical gestures
- Exact distance
- Material composition
- Sounds not already implied by a named event

Even harmless details can become canon. If you write that the Chapel smelled of wax, someone may later remember candles that never existed.

The practical test is:

> Could a later reader use this sentence as evidence about the world, a character, or an event?

If yes, the sentence needs a source.

Controlled atmosphere should make established facts feel connected. It should not make the empty space look documented.

## Expanding combat without inventing tactics

Combat is the hardest section to expand safely.

Notes often say:

- Combat starts.
- The Party advances.
- Session ends mid-fight.

One paragraph feels too small for an encounter that occupied an hour at the table. But adding named attacks, wounds, positions, spells, or enemy behavior creates tactical facts.

The solution is to expand the _pressure and duration_ of the known combat, not its tactical content.

Safe combat expansion can describe:

- That the fight continues
- That it interrupts the prior plan
- That attention narrows
- That decisions become immediate
- That the outcome remains unresolved
- That the encounter takes narrative space
- That the next recorded state has not yet been reached

For example:

> The fight did not resolve in its opening exchange. It became the session's new frame. The careful sequence that had carried the Party this far gave way to immediate decisions, each one made before the larger situation could settle.
>
> The record does not preserve individual attacks or positions, so those details remain open. What it does preserve is duration: the Party was still engaged when the session ended. The encounter belongs to this session as an extended struggle, but its tactics do not.
>
> The scene should therefore end under pressure, not with victory. The next session owns the result.

That is more than "they fought," but it does not create a single weapon, movement, injury, or enemy response.

Unsafe combat expansion sounds natural:

> Arrows struck the wagon while the Fighter held the left flank and the Mage covered the road in fire.

Unless every part of that sentence appears in the evidence, it is fiction.

Another common mistake is borrowing tactics from the next session. If the next record contains a shield rush, the model may use it to enrich the end of the missing session. This is chronology theft. A tactic belongs to the session where it is recorded unless a source explicitly says it started earlier.

The prompt needs a hard stopping rule:

> If combat continues into the next session, stop before the first tactic that is only recorded there.

## How the prompt changed after the model invented too much

My first prompts focused on plausibility:

> Use the surrounding sessions to infer what most likely happened. Write a long, detailed narrative that connects them.

The model did exactly what I asked. It selected likely causes, filled quiet moments, assigned actions to characters, and pulled later details backward to produce a complete arc.

The result was readable. It was also wrong in the specific way that polished AI output is often wrong: every unsupported transition had disappeared under confidence.

A later direct record made the failure visible. The notes already contained a clear sequence. The model had treated them as suggestions, then used neighboring context to "improve" the story:

- It added a failed version of a plan that the notes did not describe.
- It introduced motivations from an earlier session as active discussion.
- It moved details from the next session into the current one.
- It assigned causes where the notes recorded only outcomes.
- It resolved unclear possession of an object.

The correction was not "be more accurate." That is too vague. The prompt needed structural changes.

### Change 1: notes became immutable

Old:

> Use the notes and neighboring sessions.

New:

> Treat target-session notes as an immutable ordered spine. Do not replace, reinterpret, omit, or reorder a note to improve continuity.

### Change 2: sources got roles

Old:

> Consider all context.

New:

> Target notes define events. The previous session defines the opening state. The next session defines the maximum ending state. Reference material resolves identity only.

### Change 3: facts were extracted before prose

Old:

> Write a detailed narrative.

New:

> First produce a chronology, continuity ledger, hard constraints, and confidence ledger. Write prose only after those pass review.

### Change 4: atmosphere got a budget

Old:

> Make it vivid and atmospheric.

New:

> Add rhythm, emphasis, and metaphor around recorded events. Do not add sensory facts, dialogue, gestures, tactics, causes, outcomes, or character decisions.

### Change 5: the ending became a boundary

Old:

> Connect the session to the next one.

New:

> Stop at the latest event supported by target evidence. Use the next session to verify continuity, not to extend the target session.

### Change 6: persistence became a separate human action

Old:

> Reconstruct the page.

New:

> Return a non-canon draft only. Do not write to the campaign wiki. A human must approve the claims and explicitly request persistence.

The revised prompt produces less decisive prose. That is a success. It replaces false certainty with an audit trail.

## Worked example 1: boundary-only gap

Everything in this example is synthetic.

### Available evidence

**Session N, final state**

- Party reaches the old Chapel.
- Main entrance is closed.
- Scout is with the Party.
- Party intends to enter, but the session ends outside.

**Session N+2, opening state**

- Party is in the Chapel's central hall.
- Scout is not with the visible group.
- East interior door is blocked.
- Party's immediate goal is to find the Scout.
- Chapel's central mystery remains unresolved.

**Session N+1**

- No notes.
- No transcript.
- No encounter log.

### Step 1: hard constraints

```markdown
- HC-01: Begin outside the Chapel.
- HC-02: End no later than the Party's presence in the central hall.
- HC-03: The Party enters the Chapel during the gap.
- HC-04: The Scout becomes separated or otherwise absent from the visible group.
- HC-05: Do not state how the Party entered.
- HC-06: Do not state when or why the Scout became absent.
- HC-07: Do not state who blocked the east door.
- HC-08: Do not resolve the central mystery.
```

### Step 2: required and possible transitions

```markdown
Required:

- outside -> inside
- Scout visible -> Scout not visible
- objective enter Chapel -> objective find Scout

Observed after the gap, cause unknown:

- east door is blocked

Possible, not narratable as fact:

- Scout entered before the rest of the Party
- Scout became separated after entry
- Party used the main entrance
- Party used another entrance
- East door was blocked during the missing session
- East door was already blocked
```

### Step 3: confidence ledger

```markdown
| Claim                                  | Level       |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Party entered Chapel                   | required    |
| Scout became absent from visible group | required    |
| Party reached central hall             | required    |
| Main entrance was forced               | possible    |
| Scout entered first                    | possible    |
| East door caused separation            | possible    |
| Party fought inside                    | unsupported |
```

### Step 4: non-canon narrative

> **Non-canon boundary interpolation**
>
> The missing session carried the Party across the Chapel's threshold. That is the only certain direction of travel: they began outside a closed entrance, and the next reliable account places them in the central hall.
>
> The surviving record does not show which entrance they used or what allowed them to pass it. It also does not preserve the point at which the Scout ceased to be part of the visible group. By the next session, that absence had become the Party's immediate concern. Entry was no longer the objective. Finding the Scout was.
>
> The east interior door was blocked when the record resumed. Its connection to the missing events is unknown. It may belong to the route, the separation, or neither. The gap supports no reliable account of who changed it or when.
>
> The missing session therefore contains at least two transitions: the Party entered the Chapel, and the group ceased to be whole. Everything between those facts remains open. The Chapel's central mystery also remains unresolved and must not be pulled into this gap.

This version is useful because it tells a future reader exactly what the archive can support. It does not pretend to be a recovered play report.

## Worked example 2: notes plus neighbors

Everything in this example is synthetic.

### Available evidence

**Previous session, final state**

- Party shelters in a ruined observatory.
- Floodwater has cut the direct road.
- Party plans to send a route sketch across the flooded channel.
- The old Mill is the next safe landmark.

**Target-session notes**

1. Party confirms the route.
2. Map is sealed in a waxed tube.
3. Scout carries the tube through the flooded channel.
4. Rest of Party enters the old Mill.
5. Mill clock starts.
6. Guards arrive outside.
7. Session ends with Party hiding.

**Next session, opening state**

- Party is still inside the Mill.
- Scout has returned and has the empty tube.
- Guards are searching the lower floor.
- The mill clock is silent.
- The upper wheel is jammed.

### Step 1: source hierarchy

The target notes define the session. The previous session explains why the map and Mill matter. The next session confirms that the Scout returned and that the Party remained inside, but it does not prove that the return or the jam happened during the target session.

### Step 2: chronology

```markdown
| Order | Event                              | Confidence |
| ----- | ---------------------------------- | ---------- |
| 1     | Party confirms route               | recorded   |
| 2     | Map sealed in waxed tube           | recorded   |
| 3     | Scout carries tube through channel | recorded   |
| 4     | Rest of Party enters Mill          | recorded   |
| 5     | Mill clock starts                  | recorded   |
| 6     | Guards arrive outside              | recorded   |
| 7     | Party hides                        | recorded   |
| 8     | Session ends                       | recorded   |
```

The Scout's return is not in this chronology. It exists at the next boundary, but its timing is unknown.

The wheel jam is also absent. It must not be imported from the next session.

### Step 3: continuity ledger

```markdown
| Element     | Before      | Target notes    | Next opening          |
| ----------- | ----------- | --------------- | --------------------- |
| Party       | observatory | Mill            | Mill                  |
| Scout       | with Party  | crosses channel | returned              |
| Map tube    | available   | carries map     | empty                 |
| Clock       | unknown     | starts          | silent                |
| Guards      | not present | arrive outside  | searching lower floor |
| Upper wheel | unknown     | unknown         | jammed                |
```

### Step 4: hard constraints

```markdown
- Preserve all seven note events in order.
- Do not explain why the clock starts.
- Do not state that the Party starts the clock.
- Do not describe the Scout's crossing method beyond the note.
- Do not narrate the Scout's return.
- Do not narrate guards entering; notes place them outside.
- Do not jam the upper wheel.
- End with the Party hiding while the session remains unresolved.
```

### Step 5: narrative expansion

> **Non-canon prose expansion based on direct notes**
>
> The Party returned to the route one final time and confirmed what had already been decided. The flooded channel divided the work into two paths. One part of the plan would travel through the water. The rest would continue toward the old Mill.
>
> The route sketch was sealed inside a waxed tube. That small action turned the map from a plan into something that could be carried. The Scout took the tube through the flooded channel, leaving the rest of the Party to follow the route that remained open to them.
>
> The Mill became their next point of shelter. The notes preserve the entry, but not its method or difficulty. Once the Party was inside, the session's direction changed: the mill clock started.
>
> The record does not say who caused it, whether it was expected, or what mechanism moved. It records only the consequence that mattered. Guards arrived outside.
>
> From that point, the plan gave way to concealment. The Party hid inside the Mill while the new threat gathered beyond it. The session ends there, before the notes confirm an entry, a search, or an escape.
>
> The next account begins with the situation further advanced: the Scout has returned, the tube is empty, guards are on the lower floor, the clock is silent, and an upper wheel is jammed. Those facts constrain what follows, but they do not belong in this session without direct evidence.

This version has more prose than the notes, but the event set is unchanged. Every added sentence either connects recorded events, marks an unknown, or protects the boundary.

## A compact notes-first example

Notes-first mode uses the same method with no external continuity layer.

Synthetic notes:

1. Party studies three murals.
2. Scholar recognizes the river symbol.
3. Party chooses the middle passage.
4. Stone door closes behind them.
5. Session ends.

Safe expansion:

> The Party gave its attention to the three murals before making the next choice. The Scholar recognized the river symbol. That recognition did not answer every question in the chamber, but it gave the group enough to choose the middle passage.
>
> They entered. The stone door closed behind them. The session ended with the choice made and the route back changed.

Unsafe expansion:

> The Scholar remembered an old legend, warned the Party about a flood ritual, and pressed the river carving. The middle door opened, then slammed shut when the last member crossed.

The unsafe version supplies memory, dialogue, mechanism, order of entry, and causation. It is coherent. None of it is in the notes.

## Copy-paste prompt: boundary-only mode

```markdown
You are producing a constrained narrative interpolation for a missing tabletop
session. This is not a reconstruction and must not be presented as canon.

SOURCE ROLES

- BEFORE boundary: defines the last reliable state before the gap.
- AFTER boundary: defines the first reliable state after the gap.
- REFERENCE: may resolve stable identities and persistent state only.
- Genre knowledge may shape tone only. It is not evidence.

TASK

1. Extract the BEFORE state.
2. Extract the AFTER state.
3. List the minimum transitions required to connect them.
4. List plausible alternatives separately. Do not choose between alternatives
   unless one is required by evidence.
5. Build a confidence ledger with: Recorded, Required, Supported, Possible,
   Unsupported.
6. Write a readable bridge using only Recorded and Required claims.
7. End with unresolved questions.

HARD RULES

- Do not invent dialogue, tactics, causes, motives, sensory details, weather,
  architecture, injuries, objects, spells, routes, or outcomes.
- Do not infer that a visible state change was caused by the Party.
- Do not resolve mysteries that remain unresolved after the gap.
- Do not convert a plausible explanation into a factual narrative.
- Use explicit uncertainty where the method, cause, actor, or timing is unknown.
- Label the prose: "Non-canon boundary interpolation."
- Do not write to any wiki, database, or campaign system.

OUTPUT
```

## Copy-paste prompt: notes-first mode

```markdown
Turn the target-session notes below into readable prose without changing the
event set.

The notes are the authority. Treat them as an immutable spine.

WORKFLOW

1. Extract each factual note as a numbered event.
2. State whether note order is known to be chronological.
3. List actors, locations, objects, state changes, and unresolved threads.
4. Create hard constraints.
5. Write prose that preserves every event and its supported order.
6. Audit every sentence against the notes.

ALLOWED EXPANSION

- Sentence rhythm
- Transitions that do not imply an unrecorded event
- Repetition or reframing of known stakes
- Metaphor attached only to recorded facts
- Explicit statements of uncertainty

- New dialogue or paraphrased dialogue
```

## Copy-paste prompt: notes plus neighbors mode

```markdown
Produce a constrained prose expansion for one incomplete tabletop session.

1. TARGET NOTES define what happens in the target session.
2. BEFORE SESSION defines opening context only.
3. AFTER SESSION defines the maximum ending boundary and verifies continuity.
4. REFERENCE resolves stable identity and persistent state only.

WORKFLOW

1. Extract a chronology from TARGET NOTES.
```

## Copy-paste prompt: final review

The review should run as a separate pass. A model that wrote the prose is biased toward defending its own transitions.

```markdown
Audit the draft against the supplied sources. Do not rewrite it yet.

For every factual clause in the draft:

1. Quote the shortest identifying phrase.
2. Classify it as Recorded, Required, Supported, Possible, or Unsupported.
3. Cite the exact source statement that supports it.
4. Flag chronology theft from the next session.
5. Flag invented causes, motives, dialogue, sensory details, tactics, object
   transfers, injuries, outcomes, or named actors.

Then provide:

- DELETE: unsupported clauses
- QUALIFY: possible claims presented too strongly
- KEEP: recorded or required claims
- MISSING: direct notes omitted from the draft
- ORDER ERRORS: events moved from their supported order
- BOUNDARY ERRORS: next-session facts pulled backward
- CANON STATUS: must remain non-canon until human approval

Do not write to any external system.

SOURCES:
[paste sources]

DRAFT:
[paste draft]
```

## Review checklist

Before a draft is shown as useful, and certainly before it is stored, review it in this order.

### Source coverage

- [ ] Every direct target note appears.
- [ ] No direct target note was softened, replaced, or reordered.
- [ ] Neighboring sessions are used only for boundaries and continuity.
- [ ] Stable reference facts are not turned into target-session events.
- [ ] Conflicts are visible.

### Chronology

- [ ] The prose preserves every known order.
- [ ] Unknown order remains unknown.
- [ ] The opening matches the previous reliable state.
- [ ] The ending stops at the target session's evidence boundary.
- [ ] No later tactic, result, discovery, or state was moved backward.

### Actors and objects

- [ ] Group actions remain group actions unless a source names an actor.
- [ ] Pronouns resolve to supported actors.
- [ ] Object possession is not guessed.
- [ ] Object use is not inferred from proximity.
- [ ] Missing or sealed objects remain missing or sealed when required.

### Invention

- [ ] No new dialogue.
- [ ] No new motives.
- [ ] No new causes.
- [ ] No new reactions or gestures.
- [ ] No new sensory or environmental facts.
- [ ] No new combat tactics.
- [ ] No new injuries or consequences.
- [ ] No mystery is resolved because one answer seems likely.

### Combat

- [ ] Combat receives enough narrative space for readability.
- [ ] Expansion covers pressure, pace, and unresolved status only.
- [ ] No attack, position, weapon, spell, formation, or enemy response was added.
- [ ] The result belongs to the correct session.

### Confidence and labeling

- [ ] Every claim is represented in the confidence ledger.
- [ ] Possible claims do not appear as facts in the main prose.
- [ ] Unknowns are stated directly.
- [ ] The draft is labeled non-canon.
- [ ] The draft says what source mode was used.

### Persistence

- [ ] The output is still a draft.
- [ ] A human has reviewed the factual claims.
- [ ] A human has approved the prose.
- [ ] A human has explicitly requested the wiki write.
- [ ] The stored page will preserve a non-canon or reconstructed label if uncertainty remains.

## Failure modes

The same mistakes appeared often enough to deserve names.

### The plausible bridge

The model finds one likely explanation and writes it as history.

Fix: require all competing explanations, then permit only their shared minimum in the narrative.

### Neighbor gravity

Detailed neighboring sessions overpower sparse target notes. Events slide across the session boundary.

Fix: assign each source a role and add a backward-borrowing audit.

### The completed arc

The model dislikes ending in the middle of a fight, journey, negotiation, or puzzle. It supplies closure.

Fix: add negative constraints and state which unresolved thread belongs to the next session.

### Ability equals action

A character has a known spell, tool, or talent, so the model uses it to solve the gap.

Fix: reference data may resolve capability, never action.

### Object magnetism

A key appears near a locked door. A torch appears near a fire. A rope appears near a descent. The model creates a cause.

Fix: track possession and use separately.

### Atmosphere smuggling

The request for richer prose introduces rain, smoke, echoing halls, nervous glances, or shouted commands.

Fix: limit atmosphere to rhythm, known stakes, and metaphor tied to recorded facts.

### Tactical inflation

"A fight starts" becomes a full battle map in prose.

Fix: expand temporal and emotional pressure without naming tactics.

### Retrospective certainty

A later summary compresses several sessions into one sentence. The model treats it as a timestamped event.

Fix: later summaries confirm eventual state, not exact placement.

### Silent contradiction repair

Two sources disagree. The model edits one until the archive looks consistent.

Fix: preserve the higher-tier source and report the contradiction.

### Canon by formatting

The draft is written directly into the same wiki template as verified session summaries. Future readers cannot tell the difference.

Fix: use prominent non-canon labeling and require human approval before writing.

### Helpful persistence

The model finishes the draft and immediately updates the campaign wiki because that seems like the useful next step.

Fix: make drafting and persistence separate permissions. Reading source material authorizes analysis. It does not authorize canon changes.

## Non-canon labeling is part of the data model

A small italic note at the bottom is not enough.

The uncertainty should survive copying, search results, exports, and future AI ingestion. Put it where tools and humans will keep seeing it.

A useful label contains:

- Status: non-canon
- Method: boundary-only, notes-first, or notes plus neighbors
- Sources used: described by type, not private link
- Human review state
- Known uncertainty

For example:

```markdown
> Status: Non-canon interpolation
> Method: Notes plus immediate continuity boundaries
> Evidence: Direct player notes, previous-session ending, next-session opening
> Review: Awaiting human approval
> Limits: Dialogue, exact tactics, causes, and unrecorded transitions remain unknown
```

If a human later confirms the draft from memory, the status can change. Keep the provenance:

```markdown
> Status: Human-approved reconstructed summary
> Basis: Direct notes plus reviewer memory
> Remaining uncertainty: Exact dialogue and round-by-round combat
```

Do not silently convert "AI draft" into "session summary." Provenance is part of the content.

## Human approval before the wiki write

The safest workflow has two distinct operations:

1. Generate and review a draft.
2. Persist an approved version.

The first operation is reversible and private. The second changes the archive that future players, search tools, and models may treat as truth.

That difference matters even when the wiki has version history. Once invented prose enters the normal campaign record, it can be:

- Quoted in later summaries
- Used to answer campaign questions
- Linked to characters and locations
- Included in retrieval context
- Repeated until it feels remembered

The write gate should therefore be explicit:

> The draft is ready for review. I have not written it to the wiki. Persistence requires an explicit approval.

Approval should cover both facts and presentation. A human may accept the sequence but reject a metaphor that gives the wrong tone. They may remember one missing tactical detail and add it. They may decide that a boundary-only gap is more useful as an uncertainty note than as prose.

The agent should never treat "looks good" from an earlier stage as permission for a later write unless the user clearly connects the approval to persistence.

## The deeper lesson: constrain generation by separating phases

The final prompt is longer than "fill this gap," but the important improvement is not the number of rules. It is the phase separation.

1. Gather sources.
2. Rank sources.
3. Extract facts.
4. Model chronology.
5. Compare continuity.
6. Record confidence.
7. Write prose.
8. Audit prose.
9. Ask for human approval.
10. Persist only after approval.

Each phase has a different job. Problems start when one phase is asked to do all of them at once.

A model writing prose is rewarded by coherence. A model extracting constraints is rewarded by coverage. A model auditing claims is rewarded by skepticism. One prompt can ask for all three, but the outputs should still be separate.

This pattern applies beyond tabletop campaigns:

- Filling gaps in incident timelines
- Turning incomplete meeting notes into minutes
- Reconstructing project decisions from issues and commits
- Summarizing an incomplete research log
- Connecting missing steps in operational documentation

In every case, fluent text can make uncertain history look settled. The answer is not to avoid AI-generated prose. The answer is to preserve provenance, model uncertainty, and limit what the prose is allowed to claim.

The best missing-session draft is not the one that reads as if a transcript survived.

It is the one that remains useful even after the reader knows that it did not.
