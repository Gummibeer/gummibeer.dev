PROCESS, SIDE PROJECTS

The power of shipping
something small

Why small projects are underrated and how they
compound over time.

Tom (gummibeer)May 11, 2024  •  4 min read

We all want to build that one big thing.
The product. The platform. The game changer.
But most of the time, that big thing stays an idea.

You don’t rise to the level of your goals.
You fall to the level of your systems.— James Clear

Shipping small is how we build those systems.

1. Why side projects?

Side projects are my playground. No deadlines, no meetings, no expectations.
Just curiosity and code.

They help me to:

  • Learn by doing
  • Experiment with new ideas
  • Solve problems I actually care about
  • Build in public and connect with people

↘ And yeah, shipping feels awesome.

2. My simple framework

I keep it stupid simple:

1. Idea

A small problem
that annoys me.

2. Build

Timebox it.
Keep the scope tiny.

3. Ship

Get it out there.
Perfect is the enemy.

4. Repeat

Learn, improve,
and build again.

☆

This framework has helped me ship 20+ projects over the last few years.
Most are tiny. Some are useful. All of them taught me something.

3. The folder structure

I use the same starter for almost everything:

$ mkdir idea
$ cd idea
$ composer create-project laravel/laravel .
$ git init
$ git add . && git commit -m "ship something small"

# then build the smallest useful version

4. Stay focused

Every project gets one job. If a feature does not make that job clearer, faster, or more useful, it can wait.

5. Ship something small

The useful feedback starts after somebody else can touch the thing. A tiny release today beats a perfect launch that never happens.

6. Keep it going

Small releases compound. Each one teaches a little more about code, users, scope and what is actually worth building next.

7. What I’m working on

Usually too many things. The rule is simply to keep at least one of them small enough to ship.
